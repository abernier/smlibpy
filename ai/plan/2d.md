# Design note: 2D modeling

How smlibpy draws, combines and changes planar regions. The goal: one obvious way for each
task, few concepts, exact geometry (lines stay lines, arcs stay circles), and errors that say
which segment or corner is wrong and what to change.

## Concepts

There are four, and the first one exists already.

1. **Sketch**: a planar region (faces with holes) on a plane. Drawn on XY around the origin,
   placed with `.on(plane_or_face)`, combined with `+ - & ^`, turned into parts with
   `extrude`, `revolve`, `sweep`, `loft`. Unchanged as a concept.
2. **Shapes**: CapitalCase constructors that return a Sketch for the standard outlines. Every
   one takes the same option names: `rotation=` (degrees about the shape's own center),
   `align=`, and, where the shape has corners, `fillet=` and `chamfer=` (all corners).
3. **Profile**: a pen that draws one outline segment by segment, for everything that is
   awkward as a union of shapes: tangent arcs, rounded inner corners, revolve sections,
   symmetric brackets, sweep paths. It is immutable: every call returns a new Profile, so a
   drawing can branch into variants. `close()` makes the Sketch, `wire()` the open path.
4. **2D operations** are Sketch methods: Booleans, `offset` (corner styles), `fillet` and
   `chamfer` of chosen corners, `mirror`, measures. Corners are selected like edges of a
   part: `s.vertices().concave()`, `.top()`, `.nearest(p)`, `.filter(...)`.

Hull and Stroke are shapes whose input is other geometry: `Hull(*shapes_and_points)` (convex
hull, exact arcs around circles), `Stroke(path, width)` (the region a pen of that width
covers along a path: slots along lines and arcs, ribs from a centerline).

## Why

- **One pen, not a dozen arc helpers.** The curve constructors (`TangentArc`, `RadiusArc`,
  `ThreePointArc`, ...) stay for single edges, but an outline is drawn with one object whose
  moves are relative to where the pen is. That removes the bookkeeping of end points and
  tangents that makes chained edges error prone. Three arc moves cover what drawings give:
  radius and angle (turtle style, the common case), an end point (tangent), a point on the
  arc.
- **Corners are options of the moves and of the shapes**, not separate constructors:
  `.fillet(r)` after the move that ends at the corner, `Rectangle(w, h, fillet=r)`. They round
  the corner between the segments before and after it, also between lines and arcs, with
  exact tangent arcs.
- **Symmetry is a move.** `mirror(axis)` appends the mirror image of what was drawn, so a
  half outline plus `mirror(Y)` is the whole symmetric outline; ends off the axis are joined
  straight across it. The result continues to be a Profile (it can still be closed, stroked
  or extended).
- **Immutable values**, like every shape: variants branch from a common start without copies.
- **`fillet=`/`chamfer=` instead of `radius=`** as the corner options: `RegularPolygon` already
  uses `radius` for its size, and the option names match the methods they stand for.
- **Name `Profile`**: the word drawings and CAD tools use for the outline that is extruded or
  revolved. It shadows nothing that `from smlibpy import *` users commonly import.
- **Exact geometry**: the pen, fillets, chamfers, offsets, hulls, strokes and mirrors work on
  lines and circular arcs analytically (Python, complex numbers) and hand exact lines and
  circles to the kernel. Splines and ellipses pass through untouched; operations that need to
  change them say so.

## Usage patterns

```python
# 1. A revolve section: the half outline next to the axis, with a tangent fillet
section = (Profile((0, 0)).line_to(12, 0).line_to(y=4).fillet(1.5)
           .line_to(x=6).line_to(y=20).chamfer(1).line_to(x=0).close())
bushing = section.revolve(Axis((0, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0)))

# 2. A symmetric bracket: draw the right half, mirror it about Y
bracket = (Profile((0, 0)).line_to(30, 0).line_to(y=6).line_to(x=10).fillet(4)
           .line_to(y=30).arc(radius=10, angle=90).mirror(Y).close())

# 3. Standard outlines with corner options, cutouts with Booleans
plate = Rectangle(80, 50, fillet=6) - GridLocations(60, 30, 2, 2) * Circle(3) - Slot(30, 8)

# 4. Walls and gaskets with offset
outline = Rectangle(80, 50, fillet=6)
gasket = outline.offset(3) - outline                # a 3 wide band around the outline
frame = Rectangle(60, 40) - Rectangle(60, 40).offset(-4, corners="sharp")

# 5. Corners chosen by geometry
angle = Polygon((0, 0), (40, 0), (40, 8), (8, 8), (8, 30), (0, 30))
angle = angle.fillet(4, angle.vertices().concave())      # the inner corner
angle = angle.chamfer(1, angle.vertices().convex())      # the five outer corners

# 6. Links and slots from points and paths
lever = Hull(Circle(10), Pos(60, 0) * Circle(6)) - Circle(5) - Pos(60, 0) * Circle(3)
arc_slot = ArcSlot(40, 6, 60, rotation=15)
rib = Stroke(Profile((0, 0)).line_to(20, 0).arc(radius=10, angle=90).wire(), 3)
```

## 2D Booleans: kernel or slabs

Measured on the kernel in this worktree (`ai/training/..._sketches-profiles`, probes in the
session scripts):

- The kernel's planar Boolean (`boolean_2d`) gives exact results (lines and circles kept, areas
  equal to closed forms within the 1e-7 integration accuracy) for holes, shared edges, edges
  that overlap in part, touching corners, tangent circles and coincident arcs.
- **But not on operands that were moved.** When the left operand is a circle (or ellipse) that
  was translated or rotated (`ext.transform_brep`), the kernel either fails
  (`SM_ERR_INVALID_INPUT`) or, worse, returns a wrong union or exclusive or without an error:
  15 % of 150 random unions were wrong, e.g. `Circle(6).rotate(Z, 30).translate(-4, 2) +
  Slot(30, 8)` had an area of 200.48 instead of 259.27. Differences and intersections were
  right. Today's code takes the kernel result as it is and uses the slabs only when the kernel
  raises, so these unions are wrong in the released library.
- The cure: rebuild both operands from their edge curves before the call. On rebuilt operands
  the kernel was right in 1798 of 1800 random Booleans (450 per operation; the two others were
  off by 2e-6 relative, at the integration accuracy), including multi-face operands and snapped
  coordinates that make edges coincide.
- Speed: about 1 ms per Boolean against 6 ms with slabs; exclusive or 1.4 ms against 25 ms.
- The slab approach (extrude both, Boolean in 3D, take the bottom faces) is right for union,
  difference and intersection, but its exclusive or returned an empty sketch in 15 % of the
  cases.
- Many tools at once: `plate - 180 holes` took 6.6 s (one Boolean per hole on a growing
  plate); with the tools combined into one body first (they are disjoint) it takes 0.35 s.

Decision: kernel on rebuilt operands first; tools that do not overlap each other go in one
call; the slab approach stays as the fallback for operands the kernel refuses; exclusive or is
built from the kernel's own operation, not from slabs.
